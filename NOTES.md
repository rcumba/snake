# Gameplay testing notes

I built this homework through prompts, played each version, and used what I noticed to ask for changes.

## Iteration 1

While playing, I found that the page required vertical scrolling and parts of the game could move outside the visible area. I asked for the complete game to fit in the viewport without scrolling, a separate main menu with How to Play, and a more immersive fullscreen experience. The snake looked like rectangular blocks, so I also asked for a continuous body and a recognizable head.

The revised game fitted the board to the browser viewport, separated the menu and instructions from play, and gave the snake a connected, rounded body. The immersive layout worked without requiring browser fullscreen permission.

## Iteration 2

After playing the revision, I still found too much unused space and a visually basic environment. I asked for better use of the screen and a nature/jungle-inspired setting. Movement still felt rigid, so I requested smoother, more snake-like motion, improved snake and food visuals, and a cleaner integrated HUD.

The next version used a larger field adapted to the window, a forest theme, a curved and patterned snake with a subtle body wave and tapered tail, a shaded apple, and compact game controls.

## Iteration 3

After testing the visual improvements, I wanted more dynamic gameplay and requested a temporary Giant Apple bonus. Normal apples remain worth +10 points. Every five normal apples gives a 40% chance of a Giant Apple appearing alongside normal food. It is worth +30 points and lasts at most 10 seconds of active play.

I asked for the bonus to be visually distinctive and for its timer to respect pause, restart, and menu behaviour. The result has a glowing larger apple, a compact countdown, three segments of bonus growth, and brief +30 feedback. Pausing freezes the countdown; restarting or returning to the menu clears the bonus state.

I played the final version, was satisfied with the result, and asked to finalize the homework.
